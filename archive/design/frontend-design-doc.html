Plate I

Overview & design thesis

what the page is, and why it does not look like every other weather app

A phone-first single page that answers one question in two seconds: will it rain at my exact Mumbai spot in the next two hours — should I leave now?

What it is. Free, static (HTML + CSS + vanilla JS), calls Open-Meteo directly in the browser, applies a tiny calibrated model (model.json) as plain arithmetic.

Who it's for. A Mumbai person on their phone, mid-monsoon, about to step out the door. Not a meteorologist. They want a decision — go now, or wait twenty minutes — not a dashboard.

The single job. Deliver one unmistakable verdict, recoloured so the reading itself signals the state before a word is read.

Direction, one line. A Bombay monsoon almanac — पाऊस set in print, the IMD radar reflectivity scale used as hand-tinted data-ink, and rising flood-water read off an engraved gauge. The page reads like a weather diary tuned to your street.

Why this isn't an AI default. AI design clusters around three looks: (1) cream + high-contrast serif + terracotta, (2) near-black + one bright accent, (3) broadsheet hairline columns. This refuses all three. The honest material of a Bombay weather diary in July isn't cream stationery — it's rain-damp newsprint, which goes cool grey-green, so the page sits on wet newsprint #dde2dc with storm-ink #16221f. The display face is Fraunces (a soft, optically-sized old-style with warmth) instead of the over-used Playfair Didone; the reading voice is true Newsreader italic; instruments are IBM Plex Mono; the Marathi anchor पाऊस is set in a real Marathi serif, Tiro Devanagari Marathi. Colour is near-monochrome and the radar scale appears only as data-ink on the live reading — one hue at a time.

Plate II

How it works — data flow

the live path is $0 and keyless; training runs offline in CI

The whole product runs in the browser. The live path is $0 and keyless: the browser calls Open-Meteo directly, nowcast.js applies the tiny model.json as arithmetic, and the verdict paints the UI. The training path (METAR + IMERG → fit → ship a new model.json) runs offline in CI — it never touches the runtime.

Plate II · Data flow. Top row is the live, in-browser, keyless, $0 path that produces the verdict on every open. Bottom row is the offline CI training loop that periodically ships a fresh model.json — the only thing crossing between them (dashed line). A pulse travels each path to show direction of flow.

Plate III

Design principles

five rules; when in doubt, re-read them
  1. Big answer, quiet detail. The verdict is the loudest thing on the page by an order of magnitude. Everything else is whispered — small, low-contrast, secondary. If two things compete for the eye, one of them is wrong.
  2. Colour is information, never decoration. The page is near-monochrome; radar colour appears only as data-ink on the live reading, one hue at a time, pulled from the IMD reflectivity scale. Flood red is reserved for genuine flood risk only — that scarcity is what makes it mean something.
  3. It loads ready. The basic answer needs zero taps. No spinner that spins forever, no "tap to load". Within two seconds of opening, the verdict is on screen.
  4. The subject is the signature. The one bold element is the rising water — read off an engraved gauge, water climbing a Mumbai street. Spend boldness there; keep everything around it disciplined and plain.
  5. One thumb, honest voice. Built for a thumb on a phone (big targets, reachable). Copy is plain, active, Mumbai-tuned, in a weather-diary voice — "measured now", "leave in the next fifteen minutes", a verdict you can act on.

6.3The Marathi anchor — usage rules

  • Always present, every state, masthead-left — it headlines the page.
  • Colour: var(--ink) always. It does not recolour with state — it is a constant cultural anchor, not a status indicator, so it never competes with the verdict's state colour.
  • Never translated in the UI, never given a tooltip. It is identity, not information.
  • Screen-reader: lang="mr", not aria-hidden — it reads as "पाऊस / rain" (Plate XVI).
Plate VII

Layout & grid

a single column — a "rain ticket" — with water at the base behind all

A single, centred, phone-height column. Location at the very top, the verdict owning the optical centre (~45% of viewport height), quiet detail below, the rising water anchored to the bottom edge behind everything, footer last.

  • Content max-width: min(92vw, 30rem) (≈480px). The column never gets wider, even on desktop — the phone-first feel is preserved (Plate XV).
  • Vertical: #app is min-height: 100svh, flex column: header pinned top, hero flex-grows to claim the centre, details + footer at the bottom.
  • Safe areas: page padding uses max(--s-5, env(safe-area-inset-*)). Keep the viewport-fit=cover meta.

8.3"Raining now" intensity

When raining now, the accent shifts hotter by intensity along the amber band: mm·h⁻¹ ≤ 1 → #d98a2b warming toward flood red as rate rises. JS computes the blend and writes --accent as the resulting hex (crisp contrast, simpler reduced-motion).

8.4Leave-now action — #leave

One short imperative line with a ▸ marker that turns a forecast into a decision — rendered as a prominent solid-colour action element (never background-clip:text). Present only when rain is incoming. Marker ▸ in var(--accent); left rule in the accent. Shown when v.willRain && v.startsInHrs > 0; hidden via the hidden attribute when raining now or dry.

▸Leave in the next fifteen minutes to stay dry.

8.5Ground-reality row — #measured-row + #measured

A two-column row (label left, mono reading right): honesty device — distinguishes measured now from forecast. Label --ink-soft "MEASURED NOW"; value tabular-nums e.g. 0.2 mm · 3 km; hairline top border. Omit the "· N km" segment cleanly when unknown (no "· undefined").

8.6Flood line — #flood-row + #flood

A single alert line — Mumbai's signature value. Present only when floodRisk returns watch or high. Newsreader italic, colour var(--radar-flood) (the only place red appears in text), ⚠ prefix. Low risk → row hidden. The water also tints red and rises (8.8).

8.7Footer — .foot (#honesty + attribution)

Two tiny mono lines, --ink-soft. Line 1: calibrated forecast for your spot · model vN — "how we score ourselves" links to the public scoreboard. Line 2 (required): Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, linked to https://open-meteo.com/ with rel="noopener".

8.8The rising-water gauge — #water (the signature)

A horizontal water body fixed to the bottom of the viewport, behind all content (z-index:1), read against a printed rainfall ruler engraved on the edge (mono tick labels 0–40, hairline ticks) — water rises up the scale as flood risk grows, with a live numeric cursor pinned at the waterline. The one bold, subject-true element — water climbing a Mumbai street. It replaces the falling-rain canvas in the current app.js, a generic weather-app trope (Plate XVIII).

Plate XIV

Motion

restrained; reduced-motion is the default, animation an enhancement

Restrained and orchestrated. One page-load moment; ambient water swell; clean cross-fades. Every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion fallback. Gate all non-essential animation behind @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) — treat the reduced-motion path as the default. No bouncing, no particles, no falling rain. Critically: no animation ever hides content — there are zero opacity:0 + JS-reveal traps; content is visible whether or not any script or transition runs.

Plate XVI

Accessibility

quality floor, non-negotiable, never announced in the UI

16.1Contrast (WCAG AA — ≥4.5:1 body / ≥3:1 large text)

The almanac is light-on-light only where it's safe; all load-bearing text is storm-ink on wet newsprint. Radar accents are used on large text (the verdict) where the 3:1 threshold applies.

PairRatioVerdict
--ink on --paper~12:1pass (body)
--ink-soft on --paper~5.0:1pass (body, labels)
--radar-rain on --paper (verdict, large)~4.0:1pass (large text ≥3:1)
--radar-now on --paper (verdict, large)~3.3:1pass (large only) — never small
--radar-flood on --paper (flood line)~4.6:1pass — flood line is italic; fine
--radar-dry on --paper (verdict, large)~3.0:1large only; pair with the verdict's size/weight

16.2Focus · targets · screen reader · reduced motion

  • Focus. Every interactive element shows outline: 2px solid var(--ink); outline-offset: 2px. Never remove without an equally visible replacement. Order: pill → scoreboard link → Open-Meteo link.
  • Touch targets. Pill and links ≥ 44×44px hit area.
  • Verdict announcement. #app has aria-live="polite"; announce a single coherent string per render ("Rain in about forty minutes, peaking about 3 millimetres per hour"). Don't repeatedly announce the loading placeholder.
  • The water (#water). role="img" + an aria-label describing the level in words, updated on every setLevel (see Plate XVII SR-label rows).
  • Marathi anchor. lang="mr", not hidden — reads as "पाऊस / rain".
  • Pill. <select> keeps aria-label="Choose your Mumbai area".
  • Reduced motion. Respected per Plate XIV — water becomes one static line at state height, no swell, no count-up.
  • No JS-gated content. Every section renders fully with JavaScript disabled. The live demo is the only interactive element, and it ships pre-rendered in the dry state, so disabling JS loses the controls but never the content.
Plate XVII

a weather-diary voice — active, plain, Mumbai-tuned, honest

Build handoff checklist

definition of done, quality floor, and the migration map

18.1Definition of done

  • Open on a phone (360px+) → within 2 seconds a clear, recoloured verdict for the user's location, with a pill to change area.
  • All states (Plate X) render with exact copy + accent, including every error state.
  • Rising water rises/tints per state, with the cursor read-off, degrading to one static line under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Total page weight < 100KB (font request limited to the listed families/weights — note Fraunces, Newsreader, Plex Mono and Tiro Devanagari are this doc's faces; the app may subset further to protect budget).
  • No horizontal scroll at 360px · AA contrast · visible focus · reduced-motion respected.
  • Required Open-Meteo attribution present and linked.

18.2Quality floor (silent, non-negotiable)

Responsive to 360px · visible keyboard focus rings · contrast ≥4.5:1 text (≥3:1 large) · reduced-motion respected · 44px touch targets · one-handed reach · no spinner that never resolves · no layout shift on state change (reserve space for leave action / flood line, toggle visibility, don't reflow the hero) · content visible without JS.
